cases_title: "Four mandates"
cases:
  - client: "Nordic industrial group, €1.8bn revenue"
    sector: "Portfolio strategy"
    mandate: >-
      The board had approved three consecutive years of investment in a
      components division that had not covered its cost of capital since 2016.
      Management held that scale was one acquisition away. The board asked for
      an independent read before committing further.
    work: >-
      Unit economics rebuilt at product-line level from ERP extracts rather
      than the divisional P&L, which allocated shared overhead in a way that
      obscured two structurally loss-making lines. Demand modelled to 2032
      against three electrification scenarios. Four buyer archetypes tested
      through discreet market soundings.
    outcome: >-
      Two product lines closed, the remainder carved out and sold to a
      strategic buyer at 7.1x EBITDA. Proceeds redirected to the aftermarket
      services business, which the same analysis identified as capital-starved.
      Eight-week engagement; decision taken at the following board meeting.

  - client: "German medical technology manufacturer, €410m revenue"
    sector: "Market entry"
    mandate: >-
      A family-owned business with a strong DACH position wanted to enter the
      United States. An internal working group had produced a plan built on a
      distributor model and a first-year revenue target the owners found
      implausible.
    work: >-
      Reimbursement pathway mapped per procedure code, which established that
      two of the four proposed products had no viable coding route within three
      years. Distributor economics compared against a direct hospital sales
      build. Twenty-two interviews with US clinicians, purchasing directors,
      and two former country managers of comparable entrants.
    outcome: >-
      Entry proceeded on a narrowed footprint — one product family, direct
      sales, three metropolitan markets — with an explicit two-year loss budget
      the owners had not previously accepted. The distributor plan was dropped.
      Break-even was reached one quarter ahead of the revised plan.

  - client: "Private-equity-owned specialty chemicals platform"
    sector: "Merger integration"
    mandate: >-
      A bolt-on roughly 60% of the platform's size had closed with a synergy
      case of €24m and no named owner for any line of it. The sponsor asked for
      an integration design and a defensible view of what was actually
      deliverable in year one.
    work: >-
      Synergy case decomposed line by line and re-tested. Roughly €9m proved to
      be double-counted against savings already in the platform's own plan.
      Operating model designed to a single decision: which of the two commercial
      organisations would own the shared customer base. Top forty roles
      appointed within five weeks.
    outcome: >-
      Revised case of €15m, of which €13.4m was realised in the first twelve
      months. Sponsor reported the integration as the cleanest in the fund's
      portfolio at the following LP meeting. Voluntary attrition among the top
      forty was under 8%.

  - client: "Mid-market European asset manager, €26bn AUM"
    sector: "Pricing and proposition"
    mandate: >-
      Net flows had been negative for six consecutive quarters. The executive
      committee attributed this to fee pressure from passive competitors and
      proposed a broad reduction in management fees across the fund range.
    work: >-
      Flow decomposed by channel, mandate size, and vintage. The loss was
      concentrated almost entirely in a single institutional channel and in
      mandates above €200m — not in the retail book the fee cut would have
      repriced. Win-loss reconstructed across thirty-one lost mandates through
      consultant interviews.
    outcome: >-
      The across-the-board fee cut was abandoned. A tiered institutional
      schedule was introduced for large mandates alongside a rebuilt consultant
      coverage model. Net flows turned positive in the third quarter following
      implementation; approximately €40m of annual revenue was preserved
      against the original proposal.

## On reading these

Case studies are the least reliable genre in professional services. They are
written after the fact by the party with an interest in the outcome, and the
counterfactual is never available.

What is offered here instead is the reasoning: what the board believed at the
outset, what the analysis found, and what was decided. Where an engagement
changed the client's mind, that is stated. Where it confirmed a view they
already held — which happens, and is a legitimate outcome — it is not
presented as a discovery.

References from prior clients are available on request at the point of a
serious conversation, not before.
