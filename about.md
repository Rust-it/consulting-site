## Background

Before founding the practice, Ingrid led corporate development at a listed
Nordic industrial group, where she was accountable for a portfolio review that
divested two business units and redirected roughly €300m of capital toward the
group's aftermarket services arm. That experience — owning a recommendation
through to its consequences, rather than presenting it and leaving — shaped how
the practice works today.

Earlier, she spent nine years with a global strategy firm, latterly as an
associate partner in its industrials practice, and two years in a corporate
finance team covering mid-market European transactions.

She holds an MSc in economics from the University of Copenhagen and an MBA from
INSEAD. She works in English, Danish, and German.

## Approach

**Start with the economics, not the narrative.** Most strategy problems that
reach a board have already been described several times. The first task is
usually to rebuild the numbers underneath the description and see whether it
survives.

**Two or three options, not seven.** A list of seven options is a way of
avoiding a recommendation. The work is to narrow honestly and then argue for
one, including the conditions under which that argument is wrong.

**Write the disconfirming case.** Every engagement produces an explicit account
of what would have to be true for the recommendation to fail, and what early
indicators would show it failing. Boards use this more than the recommendation
itself.

**Leave the organisation able to continue.** Models are handed over
documented and owned by someone internal. No engagement should create a
dependency on the adviser who ran it.

## Independence

The practice takes no referral fees, holds no equity in client businesses, and
accepts no success-linked compensation on transactions. Where a mandate creates
a conflict with prior work, the conflict is disclosed before an engagement
letter is signed, and the mandate is usually declined.

## Non-executive work

Ingrid serves on the board of a privately held Danish medtech manufacturer and
on the advisory council of a Copenhagen-based industrial technology accelerator.
Both are disclosed to clients at the outset of any engagement in adjacent
sectors.
